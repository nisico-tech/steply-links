Visual schedule app or paper board? How to choose

By the Steply team · 2026-11-04 · Not medical advice

Both work. Research comparing paper and tablet schedules found children learned at similar speeds with both, and that each child's preference differed. Paper is best for fixed places like the fridge or bathroom and for screen-free times; an app is best for changes on the spot, outings, and photos of real things. Many families use both: paper at home, the phone when out.

What the research says

Visual supports are an evidence-based practice for autistic children according to the 2020 NCAEP review. The format question is studied much less. In a study comparing book and tablet activity schedules, children learned at similar speeds with both formats and preferred different ones. A 2025 systematic review of digital schedules found only 17 studies with 58 participants in total, so the research on apps is still small. Choose by what fits your child and your day.

Paper boards are good for

  • Fixed places: the fridge, the bathroom door, the classroom.
  • Screen-free times like bedtime, or children who find screens exciting.
  • Touch: moving or turning over a card when a step is done.
  • No battery, no setup, works for every caregiver.

An app is good for

  • Changes on the spot: swap a picture in seconds when plans change.
  • Outings: the schedule comes with you, in your pocket.
  • Real photos: take a picture of the actual shop entrance or car in a few seconds.
  • Many routines: morning, school, bedtime and weekend, without a stack of laminated cards.
  • One step at a time: a full-screen picture and one Done button can help a child focus.

What to look for in a visual schedule app

  • Calm screens: big pictures, soft colors, no ads, no sudden sounds.
  • Your own photos: realistic color photos are often clearest for young or minimally verbal children (2015 study).
  • A child mode with a lock, so your child cannot wander into settings or other apps' links.
  • Quick changes: a First / Then in seconds, and a way to change the rest of the day.
  • Printing, so the same pictures can live on paper too.
  • Privacy: no account for your child, photos kept on your device.
  • No pressure mechanics: no streaks to lose, no red marks, no countdowns that rush.

Steply was built around that list. You can also start on paper today with our free printable boards and try the app when you are ready.

Sources

  • NCAEP, Evidence-Based Practices report, 2020
  • Book versus tablet activity schedules (PubMed 28452230)
  • Digital activity schedules, systematic review, 2025 (PubMed 40392102)
  • Picture type and minimally verbal autistic children, 2015 (PubMed 24916452)
